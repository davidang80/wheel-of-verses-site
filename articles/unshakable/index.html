Unshakable: Living in Babylon Without Letting Babylon Live in You

Lessons from Daniel 1 and 3 on how Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego stayed unshakable, and how we can too.

By The Wheel Of Verses Team · 6 November 2026 · 7 min read

When Life Feels Like UNO Stacko

A few months ago, I brought UNO Stacko to Sunday School as a pre-service game. I arranged the tower on the table, and two teams lined up in front of it, taking turns to pull out a block.

The game was fun, loud, and a bit chaotic. Every time a child turned to take a block, someone on their team would shout, "No one touch the table!" Someone else would call out which block to take. And whenever a block made the tower wobble, the whole team would cry, "No… no… no… don't fall!"

Life can feel a lot like UNO Stacko. We are easily shaken by many things. People say bad things about us. We grow tired from work and from the high expectations of those around us, and often from the high expectations we place on ourselves.

All of that creates stress, anger, and frustration inside us. But it doesn't need to be like that.

Unshakable

We can be unshakable, like a fortress or a mountain. The story of three young men in the book of Daniel shows us how.

Three Young Men and a Fiery Furnace

Many people know the names Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego from the story of the fiery furnace in Daniel 3. Fewer recognise the names Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. We will come back to those.

King Nebuchadnezzar built a golden statue on the plain of Dura in the province of Babylon. The king announced that when the sound of the horn and the music played, everyone had to bow down and worship the king's golden statue. Those who did not bow down and worship would be thrown into the blazing furnace.

Three people, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, did not bow down to worship the golden statue. The king summoned them and ordered them to bow and worship the statue. This was their reply to the most powerful king of that time:

"We do not need to defend ourselves before you in this matter. If we are thrown into the blazing furnace, the God we serve is able to deliver us from it, and he will deliver us from Your Majesty's hand. But even if he does not, we want you to know, Your Majesty, that we will not serve your gods or worship the image of gold you have set up." Daniel 3:16–18

The king was furious and ordered the furnace to be heated seven times hotter than usual. The soldiers who carried them to the furnace died because of the heat. But the three survived. Four men were seen walking in the fire, and the fourth looked like a son of God.

In the end, King Nebuchadnezzar praised God and made a decree that whoever said anything against their God would be punished by death.

What was the secret of their unshakable faith, integrity, and dedication to God? Scripture reveals it too. The story is written in Daniel 3, but it didn't start there. It started in Daniel 1, when they were just teenagers.

Where the Story Really Began: Daniel 1

Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah were the real names of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.

Let me give you a bit of background on Daniel 1. King Nebuchadnezzar had just defeated the kingdom of Judah. He brought the best of the young people out of Judah to enter the king's service. In other words, they would receive the best training, education, and food so they could serve the king.

Among those chosen from Judah were Daniel and his three friends.

The first thing the Babylonians did was change their names. The chief official gave them new names: to Daniel, the name Belteshazzar; to Hananiah, Shadrach; to Mishael, Meshach; and to Azariah, Abednego.

A name is almost the same as an identity. When the Babylonians changed their names, it was the same as telling them: "I am giving you a new identity. Forget everything about your past. You are no longer God's chosen people. You are defeated. Now you are the king's people."

Besides the new names, they were given the king's food, training, and education. But this is what Daniel and his three friends did: they resolved not to defile themselves with the royal food and wine.

Daniel then said to the guard whom the chief official had appointed over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah, "Please test your servants for ten days: Give us nothing but vegetables to eat and water to drink. Then compare our appearance with that of the young men who eat the royal food, and treat your servants in accordance with what you see." So he agreed to this and tested them for ten days. Daniel 1:11–14

They lived in Babylon, but they didn't let Babylon live in them. Daniel and his three friends could not change the fact that they were in Babylon. But they didn't let Babylon's values live in them.

They could change their names, but they couldn't change their identity.

They understood that they were God's chosen people. They knew they needed to keep themselves holy.

1. Understand Your True Identity

If you want to be unshakable, understand your true identity, and don't let the devil steal it. Your Creator defines your value and your identity. Spend time with God.

You are created in God's image

Then God said, "Let us make mankind in our image, in our likeness, so that they may rule over the fish in the sea and the birds in the sky, over the livestock and all the wild animals, and over all the creatures that move along the ground." Genesis 1:26

You are a new creation

Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here! 2 Corinthians 5:17

The new you has been reconciled with God.

You are a child of God

Dear friends, now we are children of God, and what we will be has not yet been made known. But we know that when Christ appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is. 1 John 3:2

As children of God, we shouldn't live in sin anymore.

You are redeemed

In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, in accordance with the riches of God's grace. Ephesians 1:7

The most precious blood has redeemed us.

You are forgiven

If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. 1 John 1:9

You are more than a conqueror

Yet in all these things we are more than conquerors through Him who loved us. Romans 8:37

We can overcome all the suffering and all the problems, because neither death nor life, nor angels nor principalities nor powers, nor things present nor things to come, nor height nor depth, nor any other created thing, shall be able to separate us from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus our Lord (Romans 8:38–39).

Find your identity in the Word

You find your true identity when you dwell in the Word of God.

Research from the Center for Bible Engagement looked for a relationship between reading the Bible and the way people live, and the findings are striking. When people read the Bible diligently, four times a week or more, negative behaviours like smoking, getting drunk, and pornography go down by 50% for adults. For teens, they go down by more than 69%.

2. Understand Your True Goal

Some historians estimate that the fiery furnace in Daniel 3 happened 18 to 20 years after Daniel 1. One thing is sure: Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego had become important people in Babylon. They had been appointed as administrators over the province of Babylon.

They were highly respected, and it would have been easy for them to be permissive: to tolerate the worship of the statue and keep their positions.

But they knew their true goal was to serve God Almighty.
