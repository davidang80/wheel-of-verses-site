1v1 Matches: A Guide for Sunday School and Small Groups

Two people share one phone and take turns solving Bible puzzles, or one person plays the computer. No internet and no accounts needed.

By The Wheel Of Verses Team · 7 November 2026 · 4 min read

Why it works in a group

  • One phone is enough. Two players take turns while everyone else watches the same screen.
  • Everyone can join in. Both games are word puzzles, so children, teens and adults can play together.
  • The verse is front and centre. In the Wheel game the group sees the verse and its reference as the puzzle is solved, and can read it together.
  • It is low pressure. Matches never change coins, streaks or leaderboards. They only update the players' local stats.
  • The computer can be the opponent. Choose Easy, Medium or Hard and let a volunteer play against it.

Set up a match

Open 1v1 Match from the home screen and make five choices:

  1. Game: Wheel of Verses or 4 Pictures 1 Word.
  2. Mode: Friend on this phone, or vs Computer.
  3. Names: a name for each player (default Player 1 and Player 2).
  4. Difficulty: Easy, Medium or Hard (only when playing the computer).
  5. Rounds: 3 or 5.

If two players sit across a table, turn on Rotate top player panel 180° in Settings so the top panel faces them. You can quit a match at any time; the app asks you to confirm first.

Play a Wheel match

Each round, both players race to solve the same hidden verse. The reference stays hidden until the puzzle ends. At the start of every action, the active player chooses SPIN, BUY VOWEL or SOLVE.

You keep your turn when you…

  • spin a point value and guess a correct consonant (each match earns the value),
  • buy a vowel that is in the verse, or
  • spin FREE LETTER, which reveals a letter.

Your turn passes when you…

  • guess a wrong letter, or pick a letter that is already revealed,
  • land on BANKRUPT (you lose your round score) or LOSE A TURN, or
  • fail a solve attempt.

A vowel costs 250 of your own round points, never coins, and cannot be bought right after a spin lands on a value. Whoever solves the verse banks their round score and the other player banks nothing. If all consonants are gone, the player to move can buy a vowel, solve, or pass; two passes in a row end the round with no bank.

After 3 or 5 rounds, the highest banked total wins. A tie goes to the player who solved more puzzles, otherwise it is a draw. When the computer plays, its turn unfolds step by step so the class can follow and guess along.

Play a Pictures match

Four pictures share one Bible word, such as MOSES, ARK, JONAH, SHEPHERD or MANGER. Players tap letter tiles to build the word.

  • Each player gets a different puzzle of equal difficulty, 5 each.
  • A hand-off screen between turns hides the next puzzle until the new player taps ready.
  • Every turn has a 60-second timer. A time-out counts as a skip.
  • Scoring: 100 points for a correct answer, up to 50 bonus points for speed, and 20 points off per hint. Wrong answers cost nothing, and a skip scores 0.

When a match ends, the results screen shows the winner and both scores. Rematch swaps who goes first, and Share sends a plain text summary.

Three ways to use it in class

  • Volunteer vs the computer. Good for opening a lesson with the whole class. Choose Wheel, let a volunteer play, and have the class call out hints. Easy suits younger children; Hard suits teens and adults.
  • Pairs rotation. Good for small groups of 6 to 8. Pairs play a Pictures match and pass the phone at the hand-off screen. The winner plays the next person while the rest cheer.
  • Team showdown. Good for a larger class. Name the two players after the teams. Each team talks together and a captain taps the letters or tiles.

Before class

  • Charge the phone and turn the sound up so everyone hears the wheel.
  • Play one short match yourself so you know where each button is.
  • Decide who goes first, since Rematch swaps the starting player.
  • Keep matches short. Three rounds suits a warm-up; use five when the match is the main activity.

Turn the game into a conversation

The match is a good opening, and the questions afterwards carry the lesson. Pick two or three.

After a Wheel match

  1. When the verse was revealed, who recognised it first, and what gave it away?
  2. What did the category tell you before any letters were showing?
  3. Read the finished verse aloud together. What word or phrase stands out to you today?
  4. Who is speaking in this verse, and who is it for?

After a Pictures match

  1. Which picture helped you most, and which one confused you?
  2. What Bible story does the answer remind you of? Tell it in one sentence.
  3. Is there a word you only know from church? How would you explain it to a friend?

As an ice breaker

  1. What is your favourite Bible verse, and what makes it yours?
  2. Which would you rather land on: a big prize or a free letter? Why?
  3. Tell us about a time you lost a turn but still enjoyed the game.

Good to know

  • Onlookers can suggest letters, and the player taps the final choice.
  • 1v1 needs no account, no internet and no second phone.
  • It does not support online play or challenge links.
  • Matches never award coins, change the daily streak or submit to leaderboards. They only update local stats: matches won, lost and drawn, split by friend and computer.
  • Verses come from the World English Bible, a public-domain translation.
