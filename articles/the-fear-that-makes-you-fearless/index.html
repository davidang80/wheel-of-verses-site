The Fear That Makes You Fearless

What Proverbs 3:5–8 teaches about trusting God with all your heart, the three kinds of the fear of the Lord, and why fearing God frees us from every other fear.

By The Wheel Of Verses Team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Let's Ask AI

When my friends and I argue about something none of us really understands, someone says, "Let's ask AI," and one of us types the question into a phone. But there is a better AI than any tool on the market, one we can depend on all the time: the Almighty Intelligence.

God is not just a better chatbot, though. AI answers your questions. God wants to know you, and He wants you to know Him. AI gives you information. God gives you Himself.

Trust in the Lord with all your heart; do not depend on your own understanding. Seek his will in all you do, and he will show you which path to take. Don't be impressed with your own wisdom. Instead, fear the Lord and turn away from evil. Then you will have healing for your body and strength for your bones. Proverbs 3:5–8 (NLT)

Notice that verses 5–6 and verses 7–8 say the same thing in two ways. "Do not depend on your own understanding" in verse 5 matches "Don't be impressed with your own wisdom" in verse 7. Trust in the Lord and the fear of the Lord go together, and that is the shape of this article.

Trust in the Lord

In Paralympic athletics, a visually impaired runner races at full speed, tied to a sighted guide by a short cord, trusting the guide's every call. That trust isn't built on race day. It grows over months of training together.

Trust grows in a relationship. God doesn't need to earn ours, because He has already proven Himself faithful (Psalm 34:8). And in the Bible, the "heart" is the centre of our thinking, will and decisions, so trusting Him with all our heart includes our minds and choices. The question isn't whether God can be trusted. It's whether we will learn to trust Him. Here are three ways we learn.

Pray

The more we bring God our worries, decisions and plans, the more we see how faithful He is.

Praise and worship

God is "holy, enthroned on the praises of Israel" (Psalm 22:3). And "Come close to God, and God will come close to you" (James 4:8). In worship, we open our hearts and feel how close He really is.

Hear and read the Word of God

So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. Romans 10:17 (NKJV)

We trust God more as we know Him better, and we come to know Him through His Word. Faith grows as we keep hearing what God has said.

That's why we built Wheel of Verses. Every puzzle you solve is a verse you read slowly, letter by letter, word by word, until it sinks in. Our hope is that the game helps you, our reader and player, to read and study the Word of God, and that while you play, the Word does its work in your heart.

What Trust Looks Like

1. Do not depend on your own understanding

This doesn't mean Christians shouldn't think. Proverbs tells us again and again to get understanding. The problem is relying on ourselves instead of God. Some of us are anxious because we haven't figured everything out. Others are proud because we think we have. Both are measuring their security by how much they understand.

God says, "Stop measuring your safety by how much you know. Trust Me. I know the way." The runner doesn't need to see the track. The runner needs to trust the guide who can.

2. Seek His will in all you do

In the Hebrew, verse 6 says, "In all your ways, know Him." Not "ask Him for an answer," as if typing into AI, but know Him. That's a relationship, in big decisions and small ones. And He promises, "He will show you which path to take." Trusting Him means doing right even when nobody sees, and the more we trust God, the more He entrusts to us (Luke 16:10).

Fear the Lord

"Don't be impressed with your own wisdom. Instead, fear the Lord and turn away from evil." The Bible speaks of the fear of the Lord in several ways. Here are three.

1. Servile fear: the fear of punishment

Many people hear "fear the Lord" as "obey, or else." But God gave His commands to people He had already rescued: "I am the Lord your God, who rescued you from the land of Egypt" (Exodus 20:2). His commands come from His love.

The fear of punishment isn't bad. Like an alarm, it can wake us up and stop us from sinning (Matthew 10:28). At Mount Sinai, Moses told the terrified people, "Don't be afraid, for God has come in this way to test you, and so that your fear of him will keep you from sinning!" (Exodus 20:20). He was leading them past terror into a deeper fear. God doesn't want His children living on alarms.

2. Filial fear: the desire to please God

This is the fear of a child who loves his father: not "Will I get punished?" but "Will this please my Father?" In Christ we are already accepted, so we don't obey to earn His love. Because we are loved, we don't want to grieve Him. "Our goal is to please him" (2 Corinthians 5:9).

"Is this allowed?" is the floor, not the goal. Before you post, buy, watch or speak, ask: does this glorify God (1 Corinthians 10:31), and does it help others (1 Corinthians 10:24)?

3. Reverent awe: the fear that lasts forever

This is the longing to be near God and gaze at His beauty. David, a man after God's own heart (Acts 13:22), wrote, "The one thing I ask of the Lord, the thing I seek most, is to live in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, delighting in the Lord's perfections" (Psalm 27:4).

This fear never ends. The fear of the Lord is pure and lasts forever (Psalm 19:9). Even in heaven, God's people will sing, "Who will not fear you, Lord, and glorify your name?" (Revelation 15:4).

Verse 8 promises "healing for your body and strength for your bones." Proverbs describes how life generally works under God. It isn't a promise that we will never suffer, but God cares about our whole lives, body and soul.

A. W. Tozer put it this way in The Knowledge of the Holy: "The greatness of God rouses fear within us, but His goodness encourages us not to be afraid of Him. To fear and not be afraid — that is the paradox of faith."

The Author Who Entered the Story

The Almighty Intelligence didn't just send us answers. Christ is "the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24), and in Him "lie hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Colossians 2:3).

C. S. Lewis pictured it like this: the characters in a novel could never find the author by searching inside the story, unless the author wrote himself into it. Or think of a video game, whose characters could never find the developer unless he entered the game himself.

That is the heart of Christianity. The Creator, without ceasing to be God, became one of us: Jesus Christ. He died on the cross for our sins, and He rose from the dead on the third day (1 Corinthians 15:3–4). Jesus is the Guide who has already run the race and finished it (Hebrews 12:1–2). You don't need your whole future figured out. You just need to stay tied to the One who knows the way.

The Fear That Makes You Fearless

When you fear God, you don't have to be afraid of anything else.

They do not fear bad news; they confidently trust the Lord to care for them. They are confident and fearless and can face their foes triumphantly. Psalm 112:7–8 (NLT)

"Those who fear the Lord are secure" (Proverbs 14:26). The fear of the Lord doesn't make us anxious. It makes us secure, because we know who holds our lives.

So what are you afraid of? Exams? What people think of you, or how many likes your post gets? Will I find a good job, or will AI take it first? Will anyone love me for who I really am?

Those fears are real. But Jesus said, "Don't be afraid of those who want to kill your body; they cannot touch your soul," and in the same breath, "So don't be afraid; you are more valuable to God than a whole flock of sparrows" (Matthew 10:28, 31).

When God is the One you fear, His opinion of you matters most, and in Christ you are loved, valuable, and His. The fear of people says, "I need them to approve of me." The fear of God says, "God has already accepted me in Christ, so I'm free." In the words of Proverbs 29:25, "Fearing people is a dangerous trap, but trusting the Lord means safety."

Trust in God brings confidence. The fear of the Lord brings humility. And when you fear God, you don't have to fear anything else. That's the fear that makes you fearless.
