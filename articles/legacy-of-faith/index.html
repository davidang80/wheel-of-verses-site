A Legacy of Faith: Why I Built Wheel of Verses

A game for families to play together, on one phone, around one table.

By The Wheel Of Verses Team · 7 November 2026 · 3 min read

I built Wheel of Verses because I wanted families to put their phones down together, and still have something to play.

It is a scene I see often. A family, or a group of friends, sits around the same table. They are in the same room, but every face is lit by its own small screen. Nobody is being unkind. Everyone is simply somewhere else.

I kept thinking: what if one phone could be the thing they gather around?

The phone as a board game

A board game works because it sits in the middle of the table. It gives people a reason to look at each other, laugh, argue about the rules, and take turns. The box is only a tool. The people are the point.

That is how I think about the phone in Wheel of Verses. It is the board, not the main event. In the 1v1 mode, two people share one phone, pass it back and forth, and play the same puzzle in turn. There is a setting that turns the top player's panel upside down, so two people sitting across a table can both read it comfortably.

The 1v1 mode was the original idea behind the whole app. I want family and friends to really talk to one another, and the game is there to help that happen.

Teaching them anytime, anywhere

For families with young children, this matters even more to me. Scripture tells parents how the Word of God is to be passed on:

…and you shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise up. Deuteronomy 6:7

That is teaching in the ordinary rhythm of life: at home, on the road, at bedtime, at the start of the day. It is not limited to Sunday morning or a classroom.

A game fits that rhythm well. It can be played at the dinner table, in the car, in a waiting room, or on the sofa before bed. Every round puts a verse in front of a child, and gets them reading it, thinking about it, and enjoying it with someone they love.

How a game of Wheel of Verses goes

Wheel of Verses is a Bible word game in the style of a TV wheel show. A verse is hidden behind blank tiles, and players spin the wheel, guess letters, buy vowels, and try to solve it. It is made for all ages: church groups, families, and Sunday school.

The 1v1 mode is designed to keep people together:

  • Same phone, two players. Pass the phone back and forth. A large “whose turn” indicator can be read from across the table.
  • Or play the computer. A parent can practise with a child, or a child can play alone, with Easy, Medium and Hard levels.
  • Works offline. No internet, no account, no sign-up. It works in the car or at a church hall with no signal.
  • No pressure. Match results never affect coins, streaks or leaderboards, so a match stays a game and does not turn into a grind.

There is also a daily verse, a short fill-in-the-blank puzzle, and a pictures game where four images share one Bible-related word.

New to 1v1? Read the 1v1 user guide for how to set up a match and use it in Sunday school.

More than a legacy of wealth

Most of us want to leave our children something lasting. We save, we plan, and we work hard so they will have more than we did. Those are good things to do.

But I believe we should leave them more than wealth. We should leave them a legacy of faith: a foundation in the Word of God that stays with them for life.

I do not think an app can do that on its own, and I do not want it to. That work belongs to parents, grandparents and the church, around real tables, with real conversations. But if Wheel of Verses gives a family one more reason to sit together, open a verse, and laugh over a wrong guess, then it has done what I hoped.

If you are a parent, a Sunday-school teacher or a youth leader, I would love for you to try the 1v1 mode with the children in your life. Then tell me what happened at your table.
